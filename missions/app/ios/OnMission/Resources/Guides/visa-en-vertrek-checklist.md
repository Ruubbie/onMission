## 1. The one thing to settle first: does the Working Holiday Visa fit your role?

**What the visa allows** ([INZ: Netherlands Working Holiday Visa](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/netherlands-working-holiday-visa/)):

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 18 to 30 when you apply |
| Passport | Valid at least 3 months after the visa ends. Arriving 5 Jan 2027 means valid until at least **early April 2028** |
| Fee | From NZD 770. 80% decided within 2 weeks |
| Funds | At least NZD 4,200 for living costs (bank or card statements), **plus** a ticket out or separate money to buy one |
| Insurance | Full medical insurance incl. hospital cover for the whole stay |
| Health / character | Good health and character; X-ray, medical or police certificate only if INZ asks |
| Stay | 12 months from first arrival, travel in and out freely |
| Entry deadline | Must arrive within 12 months of the visa being issued, or it lapses and you can't get another one |
| Study | Up to 6 months of study or training in total |
| Work | Any temporary work; **no permanent job**, no running a business |

**How YWAM fits it**

- **If you start with a DTS** (YWAM Queenstown has January intakes: Arise, MTB, Sports, Worship; 12 weeks lecture in NZ plus about 10 weeks outreach overseas, per [ywamqueenstown.com](https://www.ywamqueenstown.com/about-dts)): the lecture phase counts as study or training and sits well inside the 6-month limit. Outreach abroad doesn't stop the 12-month clock.
- **If you go straight onto staff**: unpaid volunteering in exchange for food or a bed is treated as "work" under NZ immigration rules (a reward in kind counts, per the [volunteer fact sheet based on INZ guidance](https://volunteeringwaikato.org.nz/files/fact-sheet-do-i-need-a-work-permit.pdf)). The Working Holiday Visa has open work conditions, so that is allowed, as long as it is temporary. INZ's page says nothing specifically about religious volunteering.
- **Risk level: low to moderate.** The real limits are that you get it **once** and it lasts **12 months**. Nothing in it lets you stay longer.

**Ask YWAM Queenstown in writing this week** (their site doesn't cover visas):
1. Which visa do you advise for my role, and do you have other Dutch or European staff on a Working Holiday Visa?
2. Is my first stint a DTS or staff? Start date and monthly staff fee?
3. What's your plan for people who want to stay beyond 12 months?

**Staying longer: the Religious Worker Work Visa** ([INZ](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/religious-worker-work-visa/)): up to 2 years (4 in total), from NZD 1,355, sponsored by a religious charity registered with Charities Services that provides accommodation and support. You need **at least 2 years of religious training and/or work experience**, and religious study by itself doesn't count as work. A DTS plus one staff year likely isn't enough on its own; earlier church or youth work in NL might count. Collect evidence from now: certificates, role descriptions, letters from leaders.

**An option if you're doing a DTS first** (only if YWAM agrees): Dutch citizens can come in visa-free on an NZeTA for up to 3 months and may study up to 3 months in any 12 ([INZ: Visa Waiver Visitor Visa](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/visa-waiver-visitor-visa)). That covers a 12-week lecture phase. You'd then go on outreach and come back on the Working Holiday Visa for 12 months of staff time, which gives you about 18 months in total. The catches: you need an onward ticket, no reward-based volunteering while on the NZeTA, and the Working Holiday Visa must be issued within 12 months of the date you'd re-enter. Only worth it if YWAM confirms that DTS students come in as visitors.

## 2. Leaving the Netherlands: what deregistering triggers

**You must deregister** (uitschrijven uit de BRP) if you'll live abroad for more than 8 months in a year. You can do it **from 5 days before departure, not earlier** ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/vraag-en-antwoord/uitkering-meenemen-naar-buitenland/wat-regelen-als-ik-ga-emigreren), [Nederland Wereldwijd checklist](https://www.nederlandwereldwijd.nl/checklist-verhuizen-buitenland-emigreren)). You are then registered in the RNI (non-residents register) and keep your BSN.

> ⚠️ **Holiday clash.** If you fly Tue 5 Jan, your window is Thu 31 Dec to Mon 4 Jan. Fri 1 Jan is a public holiday and 2 to 3 Jan is a weekend, so your gemeente's counter is open on at most two of those days. Most gemeenten let you report emigration **online with DigiD** if you move alone (for example [Den Haag](https://www.denhaag.nl/nl/verhuizen-en-migratie/vertrek-naar-het-buitenland-emigratie-doorgeven/)). Check your own gemeente's page now, and make sure DigiD works first. You don't automatically get a proof of deregistration. Request a **BRP uittreksel** on the day you report, because your health insurer and others will ask for one. Afterwards only the RNI counter can issue it.

| Area | What happens when you deregister | What to do | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zorgverzekering** | Living and volunteering abroad (no Dutch employer, no secondment) means you're no longer Wlz-insured, so your Dutch basic insurance has to stop. Staying insured wrongly can lead to claims being clawed back | Cancel from your departure date, sending your uittreksel. Have the international policy start the same day | [Zorgwijzer](https://www.zorgwijzer.nl/faq/emigreren-en-zorgverzekering), [Nederland Wereldwijd check](https://www.nederlandwereldwijd.nl/zorgverzekering-buitenland/check); unsure? ask SVB for a Wlz ruling |
| **Zorgtoeslag** (if you get it) | Stops along with the insurance | Report the change in Mijn toeslagen, or you'll have to pay it back | [Belastingdienst](https://belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/emigreren-checklist) |
| **AOW** | You build up no AOW while abroad. Each uninsured year costs **2% of your future AOW** | You can insure voluntarily with the SVB **up to 1 year after leaving** (deadline about early Jan 2028). The premium is income-based; on a support income it's probably low. Decide in December | [Rijksoverheid AOW](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/vraag-en-antwoord/uitkering-meenemen-naar-buitenland/bouw-ik-aow-op-als-ik-in-het-buitenland-ga-wonen-of-werken), [SVB](https://www.svb.nl/nl/vv/) |
| **Anw** | Same story (survivor's benefit) | Can be insured voluntarily alongside the AOW; mainly relevant if you have a partner or kids | [Rijksoverheid Anw](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/vraag-en-antwoord/algemene-nabestaandenwet-anw/kan-ik-mezelf-vrijwillig-verzekeren-voor-de-algemene-nabestaandenwet-anw) |
| **DUO** (only if you have a study debt or student finance) | Repayment continues. Student finance stops if you're still receiving it | Put your NZ address and email in Mijn DUO. Outside SEPA you transfer the money yourself each month, or pay once a year (requested in October, paid by 31 December). To get a lower, income-based amount you must send DUO proof of your foreign income; otherwise expect the full monthly amount | [DUO: aflossen vanuit buitenland](https://duo.nl/particulieren/terugbetaler/buitenland/aflossen-vanuit-het-buitenland.asp) |
| **Belastingdienst** | 2027 is your emigration year | Stop or adjust any voorlopige aanslag. Give them your foreign address. File the 2027 return (emigration-year return, the M-form) in 2028 if you have Dutch income or get an invitation. Keep the NZ address current | [Belastingdienst checklist](https://belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/emigreren-checklist) |
| **DigiD** | Applying from abroad later only works at a counter | Set up **before you deregister**: DigiD app, ID check (scans your passport chip), SMS check. Add your number before you change phones | [DigiD](https://www.digid.nl/inlogmethodes/digid-app/) |
| **Voting** | You can still vote in Tweede Kamer and EU elections | Register as a voter abroad via gemeente Den Haag after you leave | [Registreren](https://www.nederlandwereldwijd.nl/stemmen-buitenland/registreren) |
| **Post** | The government only reaches you by post via your RNI address | Turn on MijnOverheid Berichtenbox. Give the RNI a reachable address (YWAM's, or a parent's for post) | [RNI adres](https://www.nederlandwereldwijd.nl/rni/adres-wijzigen) |

## 3. Health and travel insurance

- **Why it matters:** a Working Holiday Visa holder **isn't eligible for publicly funded healthcare** in NZ. That only comes with a work visa valid 2+ years ([govt.nz](https://www.govt.nz/browse/health/public-health-services/healthcare-on-a-work-visa/)). **ACC** covers accident treatment for everyone, visitors included, but not illness. Your policy has to cover illness, hospital, emergency evacuation and repatriation.
- **The visa needs** full medical insurance incl. hospital for the whole stay. Carry the certificate when you fly.
- **Check in each quote:**
  - 12 months, or longer if you might extend
  - Cover during **voluntary work**
  - **Outreach countries** if you do a DTS (some policies exclude certain regions)
  - **Winter sports and adventure sports.** This is Queenstown, and also matters for an MTB, Ski or Sports DTS
  - Dental emergencies
  - Liability
  - Belongings: laptop and phone
- **Where to look:** ask YWAM Queenstown which insurer their international staff use. Then compare a Dutch working-holiday or emigrant policy with an international health policy used by mission organisations. Ask YWAM Netherlands too. I haven't vetted specific insurers, so compare on the points above.

## 4. Vaccinations and health

- New Zealand **requires no vaccinations** for entry.
- Make sure your routine jabs are current: **BMR/MMR** (measles has circulated in NZ recently) and **DTP**. Ask your GP or check your vaccination record at the GGD.
- **If you do outreach**, the destination decides the rest (hepatitis A, typhoid, malaria prophylaxis, etc.). Book a GGD travel-vaccination consult **6 to 8 weeks before you leave** (by mid-November), or before outreach if the destination is only known later. Advice per country: [LCR](https://www.lcr.nl/).
- Before you leave: dentist, GP check-up, repeat prescriptions for 3 months, plus a letter for any medication you carry.

## 5. Banking and money transfer

- **Keep your Dutch account.** Tell your bank about your foreign address after you deregister. Some banks have rules for non-residents, so ask yours now. Keep a separate Dutch account (or YWAM Netherlands/ANBI) for supporters' gifts; the support thread owns that setup.
- **Wise** (or similar): open before you leave, for EUR to NZD at the mid-market rate.
- **NZ bank account:** **ANZ** and **BNZ** are the easiest for working-holiday makers to open from abroad. ANZ accepts an application up to 90 days before arrival; send it about 10 days before you fly. You'll need a migrant form, a certified passport copy, proof of your Dutch address and your visa. Before arrival the account can only receive money; you activate it in person at a branch ([NZ Pocket Guide](https://nzpocketguide.com/how-to-open-new-zealand-bank-account/), secondary source, so check with the bank).
- **Visa funds proof:** at least NZD 4,200 visible in your account when you apply and fly, plus ticket money. Check the current EUR rate.

## 6. Phone

- **Keep a Dutch number** (cheap prepaid SIM or eSIM). DigiD SMS checks and Dutch bank verification often still go to it. Check how long prepaid credit stays valid without top-ups.
- Cancel your contract on time, after you've changed the number in DigiD if you're switching numbers.
- In NZ: prepay SIM or eSIM from One NZ, Spark or 2degrees. Check coverage around Queenstown before you choose.

## 7. Driving licence

- In NZ you can drive on a valid foreign licence for **up to 12 months from arrival**. If it isn't in English, you must carry an **accurate translation** or an International Driving Permit ([NZ Police](https://www.police.govt.nz/node/419), [NZTA](https://www.nzta.govt.nz/driver-licences/new-residents-and-visitors/driving-on-an-overseas-licence/)).
- **Easiest:** an IDP from **ANWB**. It's valid 1 year from issue, so get it **mid-December** rather than in November to cover more of your year. Always carry it **with** your Dutch licence.
- If your Dutch licence expires within ~2 years, renew it at your gemeente before you leave, because renewing from abroad is much harder.
- Staying in NZ beyond 12 months means converting to an NZ licence.

## 8. Tax in New Zealand (IRD number)

- **Do you need one?** Only if you'll have NZ income (paid work) or want your bank to use the correct tax rate on interest. A pure volunteer may not need one. If YWAM pays you anything, or you work a side job, you do.
- **How:** after you arrive, create a myIR account and apply with your passport, your INZ application number and your Dutch tax number (BSN). It arrives in about 2 days ([IRD: new arrival](https://www.ird.govt.nz/managing-my-tax/ird-numbers/ird-numbers-for-individuals/new-arrival-to-new-zealand---ird-number-application)).
- **Tax residency:** after more than 183 days in NZ within 12 months you become an NZ tax resident. As a first-time resident you become a **transitional resident** for about 4 years: most foreign income is tax-free. **Income for services** (wages, fees) is **not** exempt ([IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/topics/tax-residency/temporary-tax-exemption)).
- **Support gifts:** gifts are normally not taxable income, but money that looks like payment for your work could be. Routing support through YWAM Netherlands or another ANBI keeps this clean. **Get YWAM Queenstown or IRD to confirm** before you start receiving monthly support.
